How the odds work: each team gets a rating from every Division I match it has played this season, conference and nonconference, using the set scores. Its hitting percentage margin (its own hitting percentage minus its opponents') sets the starting point, and results move it from there. Nonconference matches count three-quarters. Seattle U's stats page wouldn't load, so its rating comes from results alone. The home team gets a small edge in every set. The gap between two ratings gives the chance of winning each set. The deciding fifth set only goes to 15, so it is closer to a coin flip. That gives the odds of every set score, and the rest of the season is then played out 20,000 times. Tested on the 139 matches already played, leaving each one out and predicting it, the model picked the winner 65% of the time and forecast clearly better than season records alone. Ties in the standings go to head-to-head wins, then set percentage. The WCC's official tiebreakers may differ. Every percentage is rough, give or take about 5 points.
