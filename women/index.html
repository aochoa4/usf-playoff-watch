How the odds work: each team's rating comes mainly from its scores against every Division I opponent this season, with shots on goal for and against as a starting point. Conference games count fully and nonconference games count three-quarters, and opponents several WCC teams played, like UC Davis, Cal and Fresno State, tie the teams together. Seattle U's shot numbers weren't available, so its rating comes from its scores alone. Home teams get about 40% more expected goals. The ratings give expected goals for both sides of every remaining game, which give the odds of every scoreline, and the rest of the season is then played out 20,000 times. Tested on the 110 games already played, this model forecast results clearly better than guessing from season averages. Ties in the table go to head-to-head points, then conference goal difference. The WCC's official soccer tiebreakers may differ, so treat close calls with care. Every percentage is rough, give or take about 5 points.
